## 2. Data

- **Assets and frequency:** BTC, ETH, XRP, LTC, LINK (USDT pairs). Minute bars from 2020-01-01 to 2025-12-31, 3,156,480 timestamps, aggregated to **hourly**.
- **Spot vs futures discrepancy:** the docstrings say "USDT perpetual futures from Binance Futures". But `01_download.py` calls python-binance `Client().get_klines` with no futures base URL, which is the **spot** `/api/v3/klines` endpoint (`futures_klines` would be the perpetuals one). I'm fairly confident the replication data is spot, not perps.
- **Target:** the cross-asset mean of hourly log realized variance, $y_h = \text{mean}_i \log \sum r_{i,s}^2$. This is a single pooled series, not a portfolio return and not per-asset.
- **Splits:** 24-month training window, 12-month test blocks, refit once a year. Four OOS years (2022–2025), about 35,000 hourly forecasts (1,461 days × 24, less any NaN rows). The forecast horizon is one hour.

## 3. Regime models

- **RAFT (the proposed model):** a 3-state Markov-switching HAR. Each state has its own β and σ² on x = (1, logRV lag 1, lag 24, lag 168), with Gaussian emissions. It is fitted by Baum–Welch EM (30 iterations, ridge 1e-8) with the transition matrix shrunk toward a 0.90-diagonal prior (weight 0.10).
- **How K is chosen:** it isn't. `n_regimes: 3` is fixed in config.yaml. The prior settings and EM iteration count are also fixed constants, with no tuning loop anywhere.
- **Role of labels:** the hand-built labels enter RAFT in only two ways. First, EM is initialized with the training window's label frequency vector (the same row for every t). Second, a permutation search matches HMM states to labels after each refit.
- **Compared detectors:**
  - **MS-HAR:** same EM but uniform initialization; at prediction time it picks **one** regime with a threshold rule on lagged cross-sectional vol (max, dispersion, count of assets above their 80th percentile), calibrated on the training window.
  - **Hard-HMM:** RAFT's own fit and filtered posterior, collapsed to the argmax.
  - **Ablations:** uniform initialization, and a pooled β.
  - **Non-regime benchmarks:** Persistence, HAR-RV; in the supplement HAR-t, HAR-CJ and Realized GARCH(1,1).
  - **Not included:** MS-GARCH, jump models, Wasserstein k-means, GMM, BOCPD, CAViaR, historical simulation.
- **The labels themselves** (`02_analysis.py`) use rolling 4,320-hour (6-month) quantiles that include the current hour. "Contagion" requires one asset above its 95th-percentile vol, contemporaneous cross-correlation above its 90th percentile, and at least 3 assets above their 80th-percentile vol. Quoted from `02_analysis.py` lines 7–9: *"Regime labels require contemporaneous cross-asset correlation and are therefore unavailable to any real-time classifier. At test time they are applied ex-post solely to stratify forecast errors."*

## 4. Real-time protocol

- **Filtering:** forward filtering only. There is **no smoothed or Viterbi forecasting variant** and no comparison of oracle vs real-time performance. Smoothing (Baum–Welch γ) appears only inside training EM and the label alignment.
- **Refits:** annual refit on a rolling 24-month window; parameters stay frozen for 12 months.
- **Filter start:** at each test-block start the filter restarts from a uniform prior rather than from the end of training.
- **Hyperparameters:** fixed a priori (K=3, the transition prior, the label thresholds 95/90/80). The S2 supplement checks label thresholds at 92.5/85/75 and 97.5/92.5/85.
- **Preprocessing:** per training window (the MS-HAR thresholds).
- **Label alignment across refits:** done with past-only training-window labels. This is roughly our L3-style handling, but it isn't framed as a leakage control.
- **Latency:** the only latency-like quantity is the 50% decay time of cross-correlation after shocks: 1.8h in Contagion vs 4.4h in Normal (`02_analysis.py` line 232; the shock threshold is the full-sample 95th percentile of vol). This is the "signals arrive late" argument. There is no measured detection delay and no data-availability lag.
- **Look-ahead premium:** **nothing comparable is measured.** "Real time" here means the MS-HAR threshold rule must use a lagged proxy because contemporaneous correlation isn't available.
- **Implementation quirk:** `_forward_gamma` computes $\alpha_t \propto (\Pi' \alpha_{t-1}) \odot p(y_{t-1})$. That is the filtered $P(s_{t-1} \mid y_{0:t-1})$, not the predicted $P(s_t \mid y_{0:t-1})$ the docstring claims for Eq. 3. It is causal, but it skips one transition step.

## 5. Risk model

The key difference from us: **their "VaR" is not a return VaR.** It is the 95%/99% upper quantile of the predictive distribution of next-hour log realized variance. A violation is `y > VaR` with y = log RV. For RAFT the distribution is a 3-component Gaussian mixture weighted by γ, with the quantile found by Brent root-finding. "CVaR" is E[log RV | above VaR], computed by numerical integration. The benchmarks are Gaussian (Student-t for HAR-t). There are no GARCH-on-returns, MS-GARCH, HS or CAViaR models.

## 6. Evaluation

- **Volatility forecasts:** QLIKE on the log-RV forecast (primary) and Gaussian log score.
- **Tests:** DM with Newey–West bandwidth ⌊T^{1/3}⌋, for RAFT vs HAR-RV, vs MS-HAR and vs Hard-HMM, on all hours and within each stratum.
- **VaR backtest:** violation rate plus Kupiec POF only. Christoffersen is cited but not run.
- **"CVaR" accuracy:** "MAE-CVaR", the mean absolute error on exceedances.
- **Not used:** tick/quantile loss, FZ0, MCS, GW.
- **Levels and horizon:** 95% and 99%, one hour ahead.
- **Conditional evaluation:** results are split by the ex-post labels (Contagion / Single-Asset Stress / Normal). The S3 supplement uses alternative ex-post splits: test hours with log RV above the training 95th percentile, portfolio return below its 5th percentile, or absolute return above its 95th percentile.

Splitting on realized-outcome states like this mechanically favours models that forecast more variance. RAFT's mixture puts weight on the high-σ² component, so the "38.9% → 16.1%" contagion-hour result partly reflects that selection.

## 7. Main results

The repo contains no result files. The only numbers I can confirm are the abstract's 99% VaR violation rates in contagion hours, 38.9% → 16.1% (presumably Hard-HMM or MS-HAR → RAFT; the code alone can't tell which), and the 1.8h vs 4.4h decay times written into the code comments.

## 8. Stated limitations

I can't report these without the text. Limitations visible from the code:
- a single pooled log-RV target rather than returns;
- Gaussian log-RV emissions;
- fixed K and hyperparameters;
- annual refits only;
- evaluation conditioned on outcomes;
- spot data despite the "perpetual futures" claim;
- the filter misalignment above.

Paragraph we could adapt for related work:

> Closest in spirit is Kim (2026), who argues that in crypto markets regime information is short-lived in real time and shows, using hourly realized variance pooled across five Binance assets, that carrying the HMM forward-filtered posterior into a Markov-switching HAR forecast lowers 99% upper-quantile violations of log realized variance during ex-post-labelled contagion hours, relative to collapsing the posterior to a single regime. That study takes the filtered posterior as the real-time object, fixes the number of regimes and the hyperparameters in advance, refits once a year, and evaluates volatility quantiles with Kupiec tests, conditioning on states defined from realized outcomes. Our study differs in three ways. First, we treat real-time availability as the quantity being measured: we quantify the look-ahead premium separating oracle (smoothed/Viterbi, full-sample) regime information from fully real-time information, and attribute it to specific leakage channels. Second, we forecast return VaR and ES jointly and rank models with consistent scoring functions (FZ0) and model-confidence-set inference, without conditioning the evaluation on ex-post states. Third, we compare a broad set of ML regime detectors against MS-GARCH under the same information ladder. Kim's soft-versus-hard result sits within a single rung of our ladder (L2/L3, HMM only) and is complementary to our findings.

Two things to note for our own write-up. The argument that ex-post contagion labels can't be used in real time is something we should credit to Kim as prior motivation. And their outcome-conditioned evaluation is a concrete example of the evaluation leakage our design avoids, so it's worth citing as a contrast.
